\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

LLMs can support mathematics, data analysis, and scientific workflows
\citep{GuEtAl2024BLADE,LiuEtAl2024QRData}, but completing a requested
calculation does not establish that the procedure is admissible.  Statistical
work on model selection and selective prediction distinguishes estimation
from assumption checking and abstention
\citep{LeebPotscher2005,Chow1970Reject}.  ERI applies this distinction to
method routing under diagnostic evidence.  It evaluates whether a workflow
connects observed assumption evidence to an eligible method or abstains when
the target or procedure is unsupported, rather than scoring only the final
number.

This distinction connects scientific reasoning to selective prediction:
abstention is not a generic refusal but a valid action when available evidence
does not support a unique procedure \citep{Chow1970Reject}.  It also differs
from asking a model to execute a user-specified test.  Here, the method is
itself an output constrained by evidence, and an apparently precise result can
receive high loss when its validity conditions are contradicted.

Agentic systems add planning, tools, state, or role-specialized interaction
\citep{YaoEtAl2023ReAct,HongEtAl2024MetaGPT}.  Controlled evaluations show
that apparent multi-agent advantages can diminish when reasoning budgets or
execution protocols are aligned: Tran--Kiela equalize thinking-token budgets,
while BenchAgent normalizes tools, answer contracts, accounting, and
trajectories \citep{TranKiela2026,FuEtAl2026BenchAgent}.  ERI complements
these broader preprint comparisons with a narrower validity-sensitive
component analysis.  We separately compare concise and enriched instructions,
matched substantive instructions under two- and four-call execution, and a
residual unified-versus-specialized architecture.  Outcomes include invalid
selection, harmful non-abstention, claim validity, abstention, and fail-closed
regret; we claim neither first-of-kind priority nor a general single-agent
advantage.

These close precedents matter for novelty calibration.  Equalizing thinking
tokens, normalizing tools and answer contracts, or comparing single- and
multi-agent workflows is not new in itself.  Our narrower contribution is to
place a sequence of instruction and workflow comparisons inside a statistical
task where invalid method selection and harmful continuation can be scored
separately from answer quality.  The comparison supports component-sensitive
interpretation only in this limited sense; it does not recover independent
causal effects for every architectural choice.

Test-time computation and harnesses alter call organization, context, and
search \citep{WeiEtAl2022CoT,WangEtAl2023SelfConsistency,
SnellEtAl2025TestTime}.  EnCompass separates a workflow from search over its
execution paths \citep{LiEtAl2025EnCompass}; the non-archival
Zhang--Khattab technical blog frames harnesses as compositional structure for
context offloading and decomposition \citep{ZhangKhattab2026Harness}.  ERI is
not a competing harness and does not test compositional generalization.  It
asks which bundled components---instruction enrichment, call structure, and
specialized workflow boundaries---relate to decision quality in fixed tasks.
EnCompass primarily studies search over an existing workflow, while the
Zhang--Khattab communication treats a harness as higher-level compositional
structure.  Our workflows instead hold benchmark cells fixed and attribute
observed differences among a small set of execution designs.

Sequential reasoning methods likewise allocate computation through
chain-of-thought, repeated sampling, search, or critique
\citep{WeiEtAl2022CoT,WangEtAl2023SelfConsistency,
SnellEtAl2025TestTime}.  Such methods establish that additional test-time
computation can improve outcomes, but often jointly change accumulated
context, verification opportunities, and instructions.  Conditions B and C
hold substantive responsibilities and nominal output capacity fixed while
changing their external staging.  Because context and persisted state still
change, the contrast remains a qualified implementation comparison, not a
context-free estimate of ``reasoning depth.''

Finally, neuro-symbolic systems combine learned interpretation with explicit
constraints or solvers \citep{PanEtAl2023LogicLM}.  We do not claim that
deterministic routing, pretesting, or abstention is new.  The deterministic ERI
policy instead supplies a benchmark-specific boundary: once evidential
admissibility is completely formalized, stochastic judgment at the final
decision may add no value.  The primary contribution remains the controlled
sequence of workflow contrasts, without first-of-kind or population-level claims.

This boundary is complementary to, rather than a replacement for, flexible
model reasoning.  Hybrid systems can use learned interpretation for
unstructured evidence while retaining explicit constraints for decisions that
can be audited exactly.  ERI evaluates the unusually formalizable end of that
spectrum.  It therefore cannot establish that deterministic policies will
dominate in domains whose evidence ontology, admissibility conditions, or
scientific objectives are incomplete.
