\section{Full ERI Evidence Schema and Admissibility Logic}
\label{supp:evidence}

\subsection{Task separation}

\ERI{} separates executing a statistical procedure from deciding whether that
procedure is admissible under available evidence.  Given returns, candidate
procedures, and an information state, the router evaluates method-specific
requirements and returns either one primary method or an explicit abstention.
Estimation follows only after this routing decision.

\subsection{Typed evidence}

Each diagnostic produces a typed evidence item containing a stable identifier,
the evaluated claim, a finding (\emph{supports}, \emph{contradicts}, or
\emph{inconclusive}), diagnostic version and configuration, numerical output
when available, and explicit warnings and scope limits.  The identifier is
derived from a canonical serialization, allowing eligibility and decision
records to cite the exact evidence used.  Data fingerprints and provenance
references bind the trace to its inputs.

Eligibility records distinguish an eligible primary method from a weakened or
sensitivity-only method and retain reasons, missing requirements, and evidence
references.  The implemented candidates have explicit minimum-sample and
configuration requirements.  If several non-sensitivity candidates remain, a
fixed priority order selects one; if none remains, or a fail-closed rule is
triggered, the workflow abstains.  The mathematical eligibility decision is
therefore deterministic even when evidence acquisition or interpretation uses
an LLM.

\subsection{Abstention taxonomy}

Global abstention is required for invalid input, degenerate variance,
policy-defined material structural instability, or absence of an eligible
non-sensitivity primary procedure.  Claim-specific abstentions remain
separate.  For example, multiplicity-adjusted Sharpe claims are blocked
without complete search provenance and an audited adjustment method.  Support,
contradiction, and inconclusive evidence statuses remain distinct from method
eligibility, the selected primary method, global abstention, and
claim-specific abstention.

\subsection{Canonical records}

Pydantic models reject extra or internally inconsistent evidence,
eligibility, decision, abstention, and inference fields.  The deterministic
adjudicator cites evidence IDs, records conflicts and warnings, and enforces
that a selected method appears in the eligible set.  Failed outputs remain
typed terminal records rather than being repaired or omitted.  These
mechanisms are supporting infrastructure and are not claimed as independent
architectural novelty.
